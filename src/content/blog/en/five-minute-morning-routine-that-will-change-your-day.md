## Why do morning movements work?

A child’s brain needs a “kick-start” every morning — not with coffee (that’s for adults), but with movement, breath and a predictable ritual.

When the cerebellum receives smooth movement, the frontal cortex “switches on” and the amygdala calms down. The result: a child who is focused, calm and ready for school.

## The whole routine, step by step

**1. Tree in the morning breeze (1 min)**
Grounding. Slowly raise your arms as you breathe in, lower them as you breathe out.
*“You are a strong tree, your roots go deep into the earth.”*

**2. Stroking the clouds (1 min)**
Corpus callosum — crossed arm movements. Both hemispheres begin to work together.
*“Your hands are soft as cotton wool, gently push the clouds aside.”*

**3. Chasing away the monkey (1 min)**
Frontal lobe — conscious inhibition of movement. It teaches the brain to “brake”.
*“The monkey wants to steal your attention, gently nudge it aside.”*

**4. Lifting the pearl (1 min)**
Proprioception — connecting the upper and lower body.
*“You’ve found a precious pearl of calm, carry it up high.”*

**5. Closing the door of calm (1 min)**
Closing — deep breathing with hands on the belly.
*“Tuck all that calm into your tummy for the whole day.”*

## The most important advice

Do it together with your child. Your calm nervous system is the strongest regulator for your child. It’s called co-regulation — and it matters more than performing the exercises perfectly.
