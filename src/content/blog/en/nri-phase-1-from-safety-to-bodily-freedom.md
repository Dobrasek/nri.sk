Sometimes we don’t even realise how much energy we put into keeping everything under control. We watch our surroundings, anticipate other people’s reactions and suppress our own needs. NRI – deep brain structures therapy – is an evolving movement-and-music method that uses rhythm, coordination, conscious work with the body, contact and symbolic movements. The roughly 39-minute protocol has two consecutive phases. The first is active; the second has an article of its own.

## Why are the songs repeated in the same order?

A fixed sequence supports predictability. Over time, the client knows what is coming and can pay more attention to their own body. Repeating movement sequences supports motor learning; the basal ganglia are also involved in acquiring and automating them. In practice, I observe that some clients relax as early as the second session, others only by the fourth or later. It isn’t a fixed rule.

## Safety NRI

For people who need gradual adaptation, I created a preparatory form of NRI. We start in full light, and the client chooses a tolerable music volume and range of movement. The therapist guides them verbally, their eyes don’t have to be closed, and the client can ask for help, refuse a movement or stop. Conditions change only according to their readiness. Safety rests on predictability and respect for boundaries.

### 1. Queen – We Will Rock You: Contact with your own body

With arms crossed, we touch our shoulders twice, our knees twice, and clap twice. Later we add a 2–1–2 rhythm and rocking. We observe coordination, catching the rhythm and gradual automation. Some clients describe a surge of energy and feeling less overwhelmed by thoughts.

*Symbolism of the movement: “I am here. I sense my body. I can catch the rhythm.”*

### 2. The Prodigy – No Good: Strength under my own control

Slow boxing across the body’s midline, with a twist of the wrists, can speed up and slow down with the music. The client, however, doesn’t have to speed up. They notice the position of their hands, the tension and their own tolerance. It’s about the experience of activation, slowing down and choice.

*Symbolism of the movement: “I have strength. I can use it, but I don’t have to.”*

### 3. Rammstein – Du Hast: The right to resist

With dosed pulling and pushing, the client explores how hard they want to push, hold, let go or stop. Somatic approaches describe exploring defensive movements that were never carried out; however, it cannot be claimed that such a movement in itself completes a traumatic response.

*Symbolism of the movement: “I have the right to say no and protect my boundaries.”*

### 4. Imagine Dragons – Demons: My space

After working with strength comes rocking from side to side and the STOP gesture. Calm movement alternates with a clear expression of a boundary. For people used to adapting to those around them, a symbolic refusal can be a powerful personal experience.

*Symbolism of the movement: “This is my space. I decide who I let in.”*

### 5. Linkin Park – In the End: Take and throw away

Slow rocking turns, as the music speeds up, into symbolically throwing away what weighs us down: fear, shame or old defensive strategies. The movement doesn’t erase memories, but it can express a decision to try new ways of reacting.

*Symbolism of the movement: “What once protected me, I may no longer need today.”*

### 6. Evanescence – Bring Me to Life: The lotus flower

The lotus grows out of the mud, through the water, towards the light. The mud can symbolise difficult experiences, the roots our story, the stem the path of growth and the flower a new beginning. This isn’t a claim that the suffering was necessary, but about the possibility of change in spite of it.

*Symbolism of the movement: “My past is part of my story, but it doesn’t have to define my whole life.”*

### 7. Evanescence – My Immortal: Protection and openness

A very slow forward bend of the head can symbolise a return to oneself and protection; bending the head back, openness to the world and to contact. Alternating between them represents a choice between closeness and distance. It is a therapeutic metaphor, not a diagnostic sign of prenatal experiences.

*Symbolism of the movement: “I can come closer and I can move away. I decide about my boundaries.”*

### 8. Pharrell Williams – Happy: When the body is given freedom

At the end of the first phase, the client no longer has to follow a prescribed movement. They hear the instruction: “Try to sense what your body needs right now, and let it do that.” They can dance, rock, move their fingers or stay standing. It isn’t an obligation to feel joy, but a freedom of choice.

*Symbolism of the movement: “My body has its own voice. I can listen to it.”*

## What does the first phase of NRI offer us?

The individual songs create a story: from rhythm and contact with the body, through strength, boundaries and symbolically setting down a burden, all the way to spontaneous movement. The goal isn’t to master the sequences perfectly or to go through a strong emotion. It’s about the opportunity to explore how to activate, slow down, make decisions and sense your own needs.

Healthy regulation doesn’t mean constant calm. It means the ability to respond flexibly to a situation and adjust the intensity of your reactions. The first phase of NRI offers opportunities to explore these possibilities, not a guarantee of a specific therapeutic outcome.

## From movement to deeper inner experience

After the active work with rhythm, coordination, strength and boundaries comes the second phase. Both the nature of the movement and the way of supporting the client change: support, slowing down, bodily sensations and safe relational contact are given more space. The next article will be about this phase.

## Closing message

Sometimes we don’t need someone to tell us what to feel or how to move. We need a safe space in which we can discover our own answers. We can move. We can stop. We can say no. And we can allow ourselves to be ourselves.

## Professional and safety notice

NRI is an evolving movement-and-music method. The effectiveness of the full 39-minute protocol has not yet been sufficiently verified by high-quality controlled clinical studies. The symbolic meanings of the movements are therapeutic interpretations, not diagnostic indicators or evidence of changes in specific brain structures.

- Participation is voluntary. The client can refuse, adjust or interrupt a movement at any time.
- Music volume, intensity of movement, touch and resistance are adapted to individual tolerance. Physical contact requires informed consent.
- Forward and backward bending of the head is done slowly, without pain and only within a comfortable range. In case of cervical spine problems, dizziness or neurological issues, a professional assessment is needed.
- In case of panic, marked anxiety, dissociation, dizziness or overload, the activity is adjusted or interrupted.
- With children and people with communication difficulties, non-verbal signs of disagreement must also be respected.
- In case of serious psychological, neurological or movement disorders, an individual assessment by a healthcare professional is advisable.

NRI does not replace psychological, psychiatric, neurological or any other indicated healthcare. The point of the work is not to push past the client’s boundaries, but to help them recognise and respect them.
