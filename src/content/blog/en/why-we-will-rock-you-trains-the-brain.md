## The brain and rhythm — what does neurobiology say?

When we hear a steady rhythm, the brain “tunes in” to it — a phenomenon called **neural entrainment**. Neurons begin to fire at the same rhythm as the external sound.

That’s not magic — it’s biology.

## Why 110 BPM to start?

The brain needs a clear signal that the session is beginning. Queen — We Will Rock You (110 BPM), with its primitive clap-clap-clap rhythm, immediately activates the basal ganglia and orients the nervous system in space and time.

That is why we begin every NRi session with this very song. The basal ganglia “start up” before consciousness has time to notice.

## Why We Will Rock You for the basal ganglia?

The primitive rhythm **clap-clap-clap** is like a direct injection for the basal ganglia. That’s why children (and adults) automatically stomp and clap — the brain needs it.

The basal ganglia work best at 100–120 BPM — and We Will Rock You is at 104 BPM with a maximally predictable drum pattern.

## Why Rammstein for alternating movement?

Du Hast has a mechanical, precisely readable rhythm and distinct tempo changes. The arm movement, as in running, slows down in the slower part and speeds up in the faster one — the body has to keep matching the movement to the tempo it hears.

It’s not stopping, but smooth regulation of speed according to an external signal. Through repetition, that regulation becomes fixed — neuroplasticity in practice.

## Conclusion

Music in NRi is not a backdrop. It is a regulatory tool — precisely calibrated to the parameters of the nervous system.
