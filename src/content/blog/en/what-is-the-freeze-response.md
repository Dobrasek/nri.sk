## What happens in the brain during a freeze response?

When the amygdala judges a threat to be too great for you to fight or flee, it activates a third — and the oldest — response: freezing.

The dorsal vagal system slows the heart rate, breathing and movement. The body becomes rigid or, conversely, collapses. The mind “disconnects”.

## Why does this happen to adults?

If as a child you went through situations where neither defense nor escape was possible, your brain learned this as its “safe backup”. Today, even though you are an adult and the threat is not real, the old brain still reacts the same way.

**Typical triggers:**
- Conflict or confrontation
- A sense of helplessness and powerlessness
- Intense emotional situations
- Criticism or rejection

## How does NRi help with the freeze response?

NRi works directly with the brainstem — where freeze resides. Through rhythm, movement and a predictable ritual, we teach the nervous system that movement is safe.

**The “Mobilizing life force” technique:**

1. **Shaking** — gently shaking the hands and legs breaks up physical stiffness
2. **Resistance movement** — pressing the palms against each other builds a sense of one’s own strength
3. **Escape movement** — quick leg movements activate the pathways for the “run” response
4. **Vocal mobilization** — a strong “HA!” on the out-breath physically makes it impossible for the brain to stay in freeze

## Conclusion

Freeze is not a failure. It is the best thing your brain knew how to do in that moment. And now it can learn something new.
